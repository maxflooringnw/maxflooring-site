What hardwood floor refinishing is

Refinishing restores a hardwood floor by removing the worn finish and surface damage and applying a new finish. Done properly, it makes an old floor look new again at a fraction of the cost of replacement, and it lets you change the color of the wood with stain.

There are two approaches, and the right one depends on the condition of your floor.

Screen and recoat

A light abrasion of the existing finish followed by a fresh topcoat. It works when the finish is dull and lightly scuffed but the wood itself is not damaged. It is faster and less disruptive, but it does not change the color or remove deep scratches.

Full sand and refinish

The floor is sanded to bare wood, repaired, optionally stained, and finished with new coats. This is the right choice for deep scratches, worn-through finish, water or pet stains, or when you want a new color.

Signs your floor is ready to be refinished

  • The finish looks dull or hazy, especially in walkways
  • Scratches go through the finish into the wood
  • Gray or dark patches where the finish has worn away
  • Water soaks into the wood instead of beading on the surface
  • You want a different color or sheen

How the process works

  1. Walk-through and estimateWe look at the floor, talk through what you want, and give you a clear price. Estimates are free.
  2. Prep and protectionWe protect the surrounding areas, deal with loose boards, nail heads and repairs, and keep dust and mess to a minimum.
  3. SandingThe floor is sanded in stages with progressively finer grits, including edges and corners, until the surface is smooth and even.
  4. Stain (optional)If you want a new color, we test samples on your floor before committing.
  5. Finish coatsProtective finish is applied in coats with proper drying time between them.
  6. Cure and walk-throughWe go over care and cure times for your specific finish so the floor lasts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my hardwood floor can be refinished?

Most solid hardwood floors can be sanded and refinished several times. Engineered floors depend on the thickness of the top wood layer. Send a few photos or book a free estimate and we will tell you what your floor can handle before you spend anything.

How long does a hardwood floor refinishing job take?

It depends on the square footage, the condition of the floor, whether you are staining, and the finish system used. A typical room or two is a matter of days, not weeks. We give you a realistic schedule, including dry and cure times, with your estimate.

Can I stay in the house while the floors are done?

Most people can, but you will need to stay off the floor while the finish is wet and avoid heavy furniture and rugs while it cures. We plan the work in sections when possible and will walk you through what to expect.

What is the difference between a screen and recoat and a full sand and refinish?

A screen and recoat lightly abrades the existing finish and adds a fresh topcoat. It works when the finish is dull but the wood is not damaged. A full sand takes the floor down to bare wood, which is needed for deep scratches, stains, color changes or a failed finish.
